The factory state that is speeding up.

Uttar Pradesh ranks fourth in India for factories, jobs and output. In 2024-25 its factory output grew 18.9%, faster than any other large factory State and more than twice India's 7.8%.

Chapter 01 · Scale

A top-four factory state

UP ranks fourth in India for factories, people at work, output and inputs.

The ASI 2024-25 Survey counted 24,964 factories in Uttar Pradesh, 9.35% of India's 2,66,931. Of these, 21,559 were in operation. Only Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and Maharashtra have more.

Those factories engaged 18,31,165 people and produced ₹13.11 lakh crore of output, 7.93% of India's total. On value added UP is fifth, after Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka.

9.35%of India's factories are in Uttar Pradesh.

Chapter 02 · Speed

Faster than every big rival

Output rose 18.9% in 2024-25, the fastest of the 16 States with at least 3,000 factories in operation. India grew 7.8%.

UP also added factories faster than any of those States. The number in operation rose 14.5%, to 21,559. Its workforce grew 12.6%, second only to Haryana.

The growth lifted UP from fifth to fourth in India for output, inputs and pay, and from sixth to fourth for invested capital. UP used the amended factory definition, 20 or more workers with power, in both years, so the comparison is like for like.

Value added grew more slowly, by 7.0% against India's 9.6%. Almost all of that gap comes from one industry. Value added in coke and refined petroleum went from ₹5,227 crore to −₹10,172 crore. Without it, UP's value added rose 16.7%.

Chapter 03 · Efficiency

More output from each rupee

Each ₹1 of fixed capital in UP's factories produced ₹4.28 of output in 2024-25. India's figure was ₹3.23.

Among the 16 big factory States, only Haryana does better, at ₹4.83. UP also creates more jobs for its capital: 6.0 people for every ₹1 crore of fixed capital, against 4.1 across India.

6.0jobs per ₹1 crore of fixed capital. India: 4.1.

The average UP factory is leaner than India's. It employs 85 people on ₹14.2 crore of fixed capital and makes ₹60.8 crore of output a year. India's average factory employs 96 on ₹23.4 crore and makes ₹75.6 crore.

61%of the capital of India's average factory, for 80% of its output.

UP's biggest industries are also among its most productive. Each person in UP's food factories adds 15% more value than the national average for food products, and in electronics 21% more. Beverages is ahead too. Chemicals is close to India's level, while basic metals and electrical equipment trail it and pull UP's overall average down.

+21%more value added per person in UP's electronics factories than India's average for the industry.

Chapter 04 · What UP makes

Leading four industries

UP leads India in food products, beverages, tobacco and publishing, and ranks in the top four in 16 industries.

Food products is UP's biggest industry, with 22.0% of its output, and in 2024-25 UP rose from third to first in India for it. Computer, electronic and optical products come next, at 17.7% of UP's output.

Electronics output grew 45.2%, and UP's share of India's output in the industry rose to 30.79%. Tamil Nadu grew faster still and finished 2.2% ahead, so UP moved from first to second.

Chapter 05 · People

A big employer, getting bigger

UP's factories engaged 18,31,165 people in 2024-25, 2,05,419 more than a year earlier.

Workers make up 14.6 lakh of them. 62% of workers are directly employed and the rest come through contractors. Directly employed women number 71,111, up 10.4% in a year.

Pay is rising. Wages per worker reached ₹1,88,182 a year, up 6.7% and faster than India's 5.3%, though still below India's average of ₹2,28,037.

UP's factories also pass a large share of what they earn to their people. Pay and benefits came to 40.8% of net value added in 2024-25, second only to Tamil Nadu among the ten States with the most factory workers, and well above India's 34.9%.

+10.4%more women directly employed than a year earlier.

Women are UP's biggest untapped workforce. They make up 7.9% of directly employed factory workers, against 18.1% across India. Garment factories alone employ 30,026 of the 71,111 women, 42% of the total.

UP leans less on contract labour than India, at 38% of workers against 42%. Pay is more unequal, though: the average manager or supervisor earns 8.0 times what a worker does, against 6.4 times across India.

42%of the women in UP's factories work in garment making.

Chapter 06 · Money

Investing more, burning less

Factories added ₹69,336 crore of fixed assets in 2024-25, 24.7% more than the year before.

Fixed capital rose 18.6%, to ₹3.06 lakh crore. Output grew a little faster, so each rupee of capital kept working as hard as before.

Of every ₹100 of output, ₹13.64 is value added after paying for materials, fuel and services, and ₹11.74 remains after depreciation.

The fuel bill fell. Factories spent ₹39,373 crore on fuel and power, 3.6% less than in 2023-24, while output grew 18.9%. Each ₹1 of fuel now goes with ₹33.29 of output, up from ₹26.99.

+23.3%output for every rupee of fuel and power.

Power is cheaper for UP's factories, too. They spend 3.0% of the value of their output on fuel and power, against 3.6% across India, and buy 194 kWh of electricity for every ₹1 lakh of output, against 235. On average they pay ₹7.69 for each kWh, against ₹8.18 nationally.

₹7.69average price per kWh of electricity bought. India: ₹8.18.

Much of the expansion runs on borrowed money. Outstanding loans equal 29.6% of the capital invested in UP's factories, against 22.8% across India, and interest takes 11.8% of their value added, against 8.8%. Net profit comes to 4.8% of output, against 7.0% nationally. Cheaper credit and wider margins are where the next gains could come from.

Chapter 07 · Headroom

The next gain

If each person in UP's factories added as much value as India's average, the sector would add about ₹46,731 crore a year.

UP's net value added per person engaged is ₹8.40 lakh, against ₹10.95 lakh for India. Slide to see what closing part of that gap would add with today's workforce. This is arithmetic on the survey's figures, not a forecast.

The bottom line

UP is one of India's four biggest factory states, and in 2024-25 it grew faster than any other big one. It gets more output from its capital than all but one of its large peers, leads four industries, and is adding jobs and investment quickly. Raising the value each worker adds is the clearest next step.
